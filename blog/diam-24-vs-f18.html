Diam 24 vs. F18: How the Two Multihulls Differ and Who Each Suits

From the dock, a Diam 24 and an F18 can look like cousins — both fast, both multihulls, both clearly built for racing instead of cruising. Get one under your hands and the resemblance mostly ends there.

They reward different instincts, ask different things of your body, and honestly aren't competing for the same sailor. Knowing the difference before you pick one saves you a confusing first session.

Two Hulls vs. Three: The Difference That Changes Everything Else

An F18 is a catamaran: two identical hulls, side by side, nothing in the middle. A Diam 24 is a trimaran: one main hull down the centerline plus two outer floats, or amas, that do most of the work once the breeze is up. That sounds like a technical distinction, but it's the root of almost every other difference between the two boats.

On the F18, power gets managed by lifting the windward hull and sailing on the leeward one — classic beach-cat technique. On the Diam 24, the boat is designed to lift its main hull clear of the water while riding on the leeward ama instead, which is a genuinely different balancing act, not just a bigger version of the same one.

Why Your Weight Goes to a Different Place on Each Boat

This is where the hull-count difference actually shows up in your hands. On an F18, both crew are on trapeze, out over the windward hull, as soon as there's enough breeze to need it. That outboard weight helps lift the windward hull and balances the boat the way it's meant to be balanced.

On a Diam 24, moving your weight out to the wing does the opposite of what you want. The boat already gets its stability from the leeward ama — crew weight needs to stay closer to the center hull to support that lift, not fight it. It's a genuinely counter-intuitive habit if your instincts were built on a catamaran, and it's usually the first thing a new Diam 24 sailor has to unlearn.

One Design vs. Box Rule: Racing Identical Boats or a Fleet of Cousins

The Diam 24 is a strict one-design — every boat on the racecourse is built to the same specification, so results come down to sailing, not equipment. The F18 is a box rule class instead: a range of builders produce boats to the same general envelope of length, weight, and sail area, which means the fleet includes several different models racing against each other under one rule.

Neither approach is better, but they change what you're actually racing against. A Diam 24 start line is a field of identical boats. An F18 start line is a field of similar boats with real design variation between them — and reading which is faster in which conditions becomes part of the game.

Who Each Boat Actually Suits

The F18 is the more common entry point into multihull racing, and for good reason — twin-trapeze technique is intuitive if you've sailed any beach cat, the fleet is large and well established, and the box rule keeps a wide range of boats and price points in play.

The Diam 24 suits a sailor who already wants more: more speed, a tighter one-design fleet, and — for competitive monohull racers cross-training in the off-season — a boat that forces you to rebuild your instincts from the ground up rather than adjust them slightly. If you're coming from a J/70 or similar, that's usually the point, not a downside.

Not Sure Which One Is Right for You?

The honest answer is that time on both boats tells you more than any comparison article can. SMSC's fleet includes both the Diam 24 and the F18, and a session on each is the fastest way to find out which one actually matches how you want to sail.
