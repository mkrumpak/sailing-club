Inside the 4-Day Intensive Training & Racing Program

Four days sounds short until you see what actually fits inside it. SMSC's 4-Day Intensive Training & Racing session is built to take a sailor from getting comfortable on the boat to racing it, in a single trip.

That only works if the structure behind those four days is deliberate, not just four separate lessons stacked together. Here's the arc, and why it's built the way it is.

Day One: Getting Comfortable Before Getting Fast

The first day isn't about speed. It's about the platform itself — how weight and balance work on a multihull, where your body needs to be, and what the boat feels like before anyone starts talking about fine trim. Rushing this stage is the most common way beginner sessions go sideways, because everything that comes after assumes you're not still fighting the basics.

By the end of day one, the goal isn't perfect technique. It's comfort: being able to tack, gybe, and hold a straight line without every maneuver feeling like an event, so the rest of the program can build on something solid instead of constantly circling back to fundamentals. Experienced crews typically move through this quickly and spend the time on speed and boat handling instead.

Days Two and Three: Where the Real Skill-Building Happens

This is the heart of the program — repetition with real-time coaching, the kind of feedback loop that turns a sail-trim concept you understand into a habit you don't have to think about. Expect a lot of small, specific corrections rather than big lectures: ease earlier, move forward, hold that trim a beat longer.

Conditions permitting, this is also where the pace usually picks up — moving from moderate breeze into more demanding wind, and from isolated drills into coaching that layers tactics on top of technique instead of treating them as separate problems.

Every Day Ends on a Start Line

Each day follows the same rhythm: coached sessions on the water, then fleet racing, so whatever you worked on gets tested against other boats the same afternoon. What changes across the four days is the coaching focus. Early sessions cover the platform and boat handling, the middle days move into trim and speed, and by the last day the emphasis is on starts, tactics and putting it all together under race pressure.

That's deliberate. Skills that only get tested in isolation have a way of falling apart the first time they meet competition. Racing every day means you leave with skills that have already survived contact with a start line, not just a coach's approval on a quiet afternoon.

Who Gets the Most Out of Four Days

Newcomers to multihulls get a structured path from first tack to confident racing. Experienced crews, including J/70 and other competitive monohull sailors cross-training on something faster, skip the basics and spend the time where it counts for them. Either way, the program is built around your team's goals, not a fixed script.

If you already race multihulls and want to focus only on trim and rig setup, without the racing, the Advanced Sail Trim Masterclass goes deeper on that one area. The 4-Day Intensive is for building the whole picture.

Ready to See What Four Days Can Actually Change?

The structure only matters if you show up for it. Four consecutive days of coached, progressively harder sailing does more for your skills than the same hours spread out over a season of occasional outings.
