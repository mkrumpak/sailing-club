Inside the 4-Day Intensive Training & Racing Program

Four days sounds short until you see what actually fits inside it. SMSC's 4-Day Intensive Training & Racing session is built to take a sailor from getting comfortable on the boat to racing it, in a single trip.

That only works if the structure behind those four days is deliberate, not just four separate lessons stacked together. Here's the arc, and why it's built the way it is.

Day One: Getting Comfortable Before Getting Fast

The first day isn't about speed. It's about the platform itself — how weight and balance work on a multihull, where your body needs to be, and what the boat feels like before anyone starts talking about fine trim. Rushing this stage is the most common way beginner sessions go sideways, because everything that comes after assumes you're not still fighting the basics.

By the end of day one, the goal isn't perfect technique. It's comfort: being able to tack, gybe, and hold a straight line without every maneuver feeling like an event, so the rest of the program can build on something solid instead of constantly circling back to fundamentals.

Days Two and Three: Where the Real Skill-Building Happens

This is the heart of the program — repetition with real-time coaching, the kind of feedback loop that turns a sail-trim concept you understand into a habit you don't have to think about. Expect a lot of small, specific corrections rather than big lectures: ease earlier, move forward, hold that trim a beat longer.

Conditions permitting, this is also where the pace usually picks up — moving from moderate breeze into more demanding wind, and from isolated drills into short, structured racing scenarios that start layering tactics on top of technique instead of treating them as separate problems.

Day Four: Putting It Together in Racing

The program's name has two parts for a reason. Day four is racing, not a demonstration — using everything built over the previous three days under actual race pressure, where trim mistakes and hesitation show up immediately against another boat instead of just against the clock.

That's deliberate. Skills that only get tested in isolation have a way of falling apart the first time they meet competition. Racing on the last day means you leave with skills that have already survived contact with a startline, not just a coach's approval on a quiet afternoon.

Who Gets the Most Out of Four Days

This program is built as SMSC's most complete introduction — a good fit whether you're new to multihulls entirely or you've sailed one before but want a structured block of time to improve, rather than just log hours on the water.

It's less suited to someone who's already racing competitively and wants to fine-tune specific details rather than build from the ground up — that's more the territory of the Advanced Sail Trim Masterclass. The 4-Day Intensive is for building the whole picture, not polishing one corner of it.

Ready to See What Four Days Can Actually Change?

The structure only matters if you show up for it. Four consecutive days of coached, progressively harder sailing does more for your skills than the same hours spread out over a season of occasional outings.
