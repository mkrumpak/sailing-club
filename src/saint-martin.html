The Island

One island, two nations, and wind you can actually count on. Here's the place SMSC calls home.

One Island, Two Nations

Saint Martin is small — just 37 square miles — but it's shared by two countries, and it's the smallest inhabited island on Earth split this way. There's no fence and no checkpoint between them, just a shift in language, currency, and cooking: cross from the French side to the Dutch side and you'll go from croissants and café tables in Marigot to a lively Caribbean-Dutch waterfront in Philipsburg, all in the same afternoon.

The Sailing Conditions

Here's what actually makes Saint Martin worth flying down for: the wind shows up, and it keeps showing up. Northeasterly trade winds blow through the island most of the year — typically 15 to 20 knots, day after day, without the fickle shifts that make some places a gamble. That kind of consistency means you can actually work on something specific — mast rotation, a start sequence, a mark rounding — instead of just reacting to whatever the breeze throws at you.

You'll also get two very different kinds of water to play with. Simpson Bay Lagoon, one of the largest saltwater lagoons in the Caribbean, sits right in the middle of the island and straddles the French-Dutch border — flat, warm, and sheltered, perfect for boat handling and race starts. Step outside it and you're in open Atlantic and Caribbean water, with Anguilla and St. Barths both a short sail away whenever you're ready for a longer passage or some inter-island racing.

The good sailing runs roughly November through June, which is exactly when SMSC's training clinics and racing calendar happen. We'll say it plainly, though, because we'd rather you know: this is hurricane country, and the official season runs June through November, with the real risk sitting in August through October. That's not a reason to stay away — it's just why the season here has the shape it does, and why we build our calendar around it.

Prevailing Winds

Locals call the winter trades — December through February — the "Christmas Winds," and they're a dependable way to start the season: brisk, steady, blowing in from the northeast day after day. But the wind actually keeps building from there, and June and July are typically the two strongest months of the year on the island. Then comes the real lull: September and October are consistently the calmest stretch, right in the heart of hurricane season, before the trades build back up through November and December.

Through it all, a passing tropical wave or storm system can still spike wind speed and swing the direction toward the southeast with little warning — the one exception to how dependable the rest of the year is.

Windward vs. Leeward

Because that easterly flow is so consistent, the two coasts feel like different sailing venues entirely.

Windward — Orient Bay, Oyster Pond. Facing the open Atlantic head-on: stronger, uninterrupted breeze and real chop.

Leeward — Simpson Bay, Terres Basses. Sheltered by the island's own hills: flatter, calmer water close to shore.

Home of the Heineken Regatta

Saint Martin's reputation isn't just about the wind, either — this island knows how to race. It's hosted the St. Maarten Heineken Regatta since 1980, and these days it's the largest regatta in the Caribbean, pulling in well over 100 boats from 20-plus countries every March, all racing under the regatta's own motto: "Serious Fun." Decades of hosting an event like that leaves a mark — real race committees, real course management, a whole community of sailors who take the racing seriously without ever losing the island's spirit. SMSC's own calendar of World Diam 24 Tour stops and multihull events is built on that same foundation: the same wind, the same racing culture, the same island.

Getting Here

Getting here is easier than you'd think for a small Caribbean island. Princess Juliana International Airport (SXM) has direct flights from major hubs across the US and Canada, and once you land, you're close — most of the island's marinas and sailing infrastructure sit just a few minutes from the airport, tucked around Simpson Bay Lagoon. You could be on a plane in the morning and on the water by afternoon.

Whether you're chasing your first hull-flying moment or your next regatta trophy, there's a place for you here.
